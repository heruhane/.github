## 19. The licensing standard: MIT for code, CC BY-SA 4.0 for books and question banks

*Decided 30 October 2026, in standards version 2026-10-30.1.*

**Decided.** Code is MIT, and books and question banks are CC BY-SA 4.0. A repository with both keeps them in `LICENSE` for the content and `LICENSE-CODE` for the code. Anything else, including lessons, needs a decision. Third-party content has its source and licence recorded from its first use, and anything distributed to users has a compatible licence. A repository has its own licences by the time it goes public.

MIT is short and widely understood, and is already the licence of this repository. Share-alike keeps teaching material, and what others build from it, open to learners. It also allows building on share-alike datasets, such as [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/license.html). Where something came from is hard to work out later, so it is recorded from the start.

Two alternatives were considered. **Apache-2.0 for code** has an explicit patent grant, but it is longer, and small tools and sites gain little from it. **CC BY 4.0 for content** is more permissive, but it would not be available for material built on share-alike data.

A default for lessons was left open, so a repository with lessons records its own decision.

**Obligations.**

- **Follow the [licensing standard](../STANDARDS.md#licensing-standard)**, and check a dataset's licence before using it.
